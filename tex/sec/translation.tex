\section{Translation}
\label{sec:translation}

The calculus presented in \cref{sec:expr-proc,sec:types,sec:semantics}
records ownership and ordering in a linear type system and, dynamically, in
binder groups.  This section defines an untyped run-time calculus that is
closer to an actual implementation of splitting and borrowing.  The
run-time calculus provides the tools, ordinary channels
and synchronization cells, so that translated well-typed source processes
preserve the ordering guarantees established by the type system and the direct
semantics of \cref{sec:semantics}.

\Cref{fig:unr-syntax} defines an untyped run-time configuration $(C,S,E)$.
Here $C$ is a finite set of primitive channel names, $S:C\to\mathsf{Channel}$
maps each channel to its current state, and $E$ is a finite multiset of untyped
expressions executing concurrently.  A channel state records whether the
channel is open and a sequence of synchronization flags for each of its two
endpoints.  A flag in state $\FlagInit$ records that the corresponding $\CDrop$
is still pending; after the drop, its state is $\FlagDropped$ and an $\CAcq$
may proceed.  The term forms for constants and ordinary expressions are as
before; the multiset $E$ represents parallel execution.

We call the channels of the target calculus \emph{primitive channels} to
distinguish them from (translated) source channels.  A
translated source channel is represented by a value
$\targettermcolor{c}=\USHandle{\TV[1]}{\TEVary}{\TV[2]}$, a triple consisting of an
optional pre-synchronization reference $\TV[1]$, a primitive channel endpoint
$\TEVary$, and an optional post-synchronization reference $\TV[2]$.  The default
optional component is $\USUnit$.  A nontrivial reference identifies a flag in
the list belonging to $\TEVary$.  Its pre-flag is consumed by
$\USConst{acquire}$ before the endpoint is used; its post-flag is signaled by
$\USConst{drop}$ when ownership of
the endpoint prefix is released.  Communication, choice, and close operate on
the primitive channel; the flags encode
the ordering constraints on remote borrows that source binder groups
enforce directly. Local borrows do not use this machinery.

Permuting concurrent expressions or channel entries does not change a
configuration: $E$ is a multiset, $C$ is a set, and $S$ is keyed by channel
name.  There are no channel or synchronization binders whose scopes need to
be rearranged.  Up to consistent renaming of fresh names, structural
congruence boils down to equality of configurations, and reduction needs no
separate congruence rule.

For $S(c)=(b,F_0,F_1)$, define $\operatorname{live}(S,c)=b$ and
$\operatorname{flags}(S,c,s)=F_s$, where $s\in\{0,1\}$ selects one of the two
endpoints $c^s$ and $\bar s=1-s$ selects the other.  The notation
$S[c,s\mapsto F]$ updates only that endpoint's flag sequence.  For the rules,
we give each occurrence in a flag sequence a distinct name.  In a well-formed
handle, its post-component $v$ is
either $\USUnit$ or a reference to a flag on the same endpoint.  We write
$\operatorname{ins}(F,v,z\!:\!\FlagInit)$ for insertion immediately before
the flag named by $v$, or at the end of $F$ when $v=\USUnit$.  Thus the
displayed remote-split rule has a determined insertion point.

\Cref{fig:unr-process-reduction} gives the reduction relation on configurations.
The pattern $E\uplus\USMulti{\TE}$ selects one occurrence of $\TE$ from the multiset;
the communication, choice, and close rules select two distinct occurrences.
We write $\mathcal F[e]$ for an expression in an evaluation context.  The
metavariable $v$ ranges over syntactic values, so no separate value premise is
needed.  In the drop and acquire rules, the name $z$ in the selected handle
identifies a unique cell in $\operatorname{flags}(S,c,s)$; the displayed
sequence decomposition therefore fixes the cell to update or remove.

The expression step and discard leave the channel state unchanged; fork adds
one expression to the soup.  New adds an open channel with an already-acquired
flag on each endpoint.  A local split shares its endpoint between the two
resulting handles, whereas a remote split inserts a fresh $\FlagInit$ flag
between them.  Drop changes that flag to $\FlagDropped$; acquire removes it
once it is ready.  Communication and choice act on opposite endpoints of an
open channel.  Close requires both flag sequences to be empty and marks the
channel closed.  These steps operate directly on the selected expressions and
channel state, so no parallel or binder closure rules are needed.

\Cref{fig:translation} defines the translation as a configuration-valued
function $\USTransProc{\Proc}$.  The substitution $\Sub$ maps free source
variables to target expressions; entries for endpoint variables are channel
triples.  The expression translation $\USTransExpr{\E}$ replaces free
variables according to $\Sub$ and preserves the other expression forms.  An
expression process contributes this translated expression to the multiset.
Parallel composition combines channel sets and maps and takes the multiset
union of expressions.  The channel names chosen for the two components are
disjoint.  A restriction chooses a fresh primitive channel $c$, translates
its two binder groups for endpoints $c^0$ and $c^1$, then translates the body
under the substitutions they produce.  It adds $c$ to $C$ and records the
two resulting flag sequences in $S(c)$; it adds no expression of its own.

The binder-group function $\mathcal B$ returns a finite substitution
$\Sub_B$ whose domain is exactly the variables bound by $\BindGroup$, together
with an ordered flag sequence.  Each binding maps a source variable to the
target expression for its share of endpoint $c^s$.  Its helper $\mathcal L$
distributes a channel triple
across the variables in one sequential segment.  A sole variable receives
the full triple.  With several variables, the first receives the pre-reference,
the last receives the post-reference, and intermediate variables receive neither;
their unused components are $\USUnit$.  Each separator introduces a fresh flag name $z$.
The segment on its left uses $z$ as its post-reference, and the remaining
binder group uses $z$ as its pre-reference.  The flags appear in separator
order.  The function $\BindFlag{\Bind}$ initializes the flag to
$\FlagDropped$ when the left segment is empty, since no drop is pending, and
to $\FlagInit$ otherwise.  The unions in the figure are unions of
substitutions with disjoint domains: the two endpoint groups bind different
variables, and restriction extends the incoming $\Sub$ with their bindings
before translating its body.  Thus the translated channel state records
exactly the remote-borrow ordering imposed by the source binder groups.

The simulation result for this translation is stated in
\cref{sec:metatheory}.
Every reduction of a well-typed source process is matched by target reductions
of its translation.
