\section{Translation}
\label{sec:translation}

The calculus presented in \cref{sec:expr-proc,sec:types,sec:semantics}
records ownership and ordering in a linear type system and, dynamically, in
binder groups.  This section defines an untyped run-time calculus that is
closer to an actual implementation of splitting and borrowing.  The
run-time calculus provides the tools, ordinary channels
and synchronization cells, so that translated well-typed source processes
preserve the ordering guarantees established by the type system and the direct
semantics of \cref{sec:semantics}.

\Cref{fig:unr-syntax} defines an untyped run-time configuration $(C,S,E)$.
Here $C$ is a finite set of primitive channel names, $S:C\to\mathsf{Channel}$
maps each channel to its current state, and $E$ is a finite multiset of untyped
expressions executing concurrently.  A channel state records whether the
channel is open and a sequence of synchronization flags for each of its two
endpoints.  A flag in state $\FlagInit$ records that the corresponding $\CDrop$
is still pending; after the drop, its state is $\FlagDropped$ and an $\CAcq$
may proceed.  The term forms for constants and ordinary expressions are as
before; the target remote-split instruction remains unary and always
allocates its fresh flag at the front of the endpoint's list.
The multiset $E$ represents parallel execution.

We call the channels of the target calculus \emph{primitive channels} to
distinguish them from (translated) source channels.  A
translated source channel is represented by a value
$\targettermcolor{c}=\USHandle{\TV[1]}{\TEVary}{\TV[2]}$, a triple consisting of an
optional pre-synchronization reference $\TV[1]$, a primitive channel endpoint
$\TEVary$, and an optional post-synchronization reference $\TV[2]$.  The default
optional component is $\USUnit$.  A nontrivial reference identifies a flag in
the list belonging to $\TEVary$.  Its pre-flag is consumed by
$\USConst{acquire}$ before the endpoint is used; its post-flag is signaled by
$\USConst{drop}$ when ownership of
the endpoint prefix is released.  Communication, choice, and close operate on
the primitive channel; the flag references encode
the ordering constraints on remote borrows that source binder groups
enforce directly. Local borrows do not use this machinery.

Permuting concurrent expressions or channel entries does not change a
configuration: $E$ is a multiset, $C$ is a set, and $S$ is keyed by channel
name.  There are no channel or synchronization binders whose scopes need to
be rearranged.  Up to consistent renaming of fresh names, structural
congruence boils down to equality of configurations, and reduction needs no
separate congruence rule.

For $S(c)=(b,F_0,F_1)$, define $\operatorname{live}(S,c)=b$ and
$\operatorname{flags}(S,c,s)=F_s$, where $s\in\{0,1\}$ selects one of the two
endpoints $c^s$ and $\bar s=1-s$ selects the other.  The notation
$S[c,s\mapsto F]$ updates only that endpoint's flag sequence.  For the rules,
we give each occurrence in a flag sequence a distinct name.  In a well-formed
handle, its post-component $v$ is either $\USUnit$ or a reference to a flag on
the same endpoint.  Remote split allocates a fresh flag at the front of that endpoint's list.
The pre- and post-references in the resulting handles encode the dependency;
physical flag-list order is an addressing convention, rather than source
session order.  In the positional Agda representation, all existing
references on the endpoint are shifted consistently when its front cell is
inserted.  The instruction itself has no position argument.

\Cref{fig:unr-process-reduction} gives the reduction relation on configurations.
The pattern $E\uplus\USMulti{\TE}$ selects one occurrence of $\TE$ from the multiset;
the communication, choice, and close rules select two distinct occurrences.
We write $\mathcal F[e]$ for an expression in an evaluation context.  The
metavariable $v$ ranges over syntactic values, so no separate value premise is
needed.  The remote-split rule allocates its new cell at the front of
the selected endpoint's flag list.
In the drop and acquire rules, the name $z$ in the selected handle identifies
a unique cell in $\operatorname{flags}(S,c,s)$; the displayed sequence
decomposition therefore fixes the cell to update or remove.

The expression step and discard leave the channel state unchanged; fork adds
one expression to the soup.  New adds an open channel with an already-acquired
flag on each endpoint.  A local split shares its endpoint between the two
resulting handles, whereas a remote split inserts a fresh $\FlagInit$ flag at
the front of the endpoint's list.  Drop changes that flag to
$\FlagDropped$; acquire removes it once it is ready.  Communication and choice
act on opposite endpoints of an open channel.  Close requires both flag
sequences to be empty and marks the channel closed.  These steps operate
directly on the selected expressions and channel state, so no parallel or
binder closure rules are needed.

\Cref{fig:translation} defines a configuration-valued translation
$\USTransProc{\Proc}$.  Its environment $\Sub$ maps free source variables to
target expressions, with plain channel triples for endpoint variables.
An expression process contributes $\USTransExpr{\E}$ to the multiset.
Parallel composition combines disjoint channel sets and maps and takes the
multiset union of expressions.  A restriction chooses a fresh primitive
channel $c$, translates its two binder groups for endpoints $c^0$ and $c^1$,
and translates the body under the substitutions they produce.  It records
the resulting flag sequences in $S(c)$ and adds no expression of its own.

The binder-group function $\mathcal B$ returns a substitution whose domain
is exactly the bound variables, together with an ordered flag sequence.
Its helper $\mathcal L$ distributes a plain channel triple across one
sequential segment: the first variable receives the pre-reference, the last
the post-reference, and intermediate variables receive neither.  A sole
variable receives both.  Each separator introduces a fresh flag name $z$;
the left segment uses $z$ as its post-reference and the remaining group uses
it as its pre-reference.  The flags initially occur in separator order.
$\BindFlag{\Bind}$ initializes a flag to $\FlagDropped$ when its left segment
is empty, and to $\FlagInit$ otherwise.  Substitution unions have disjoint
domains: restriction extends the incoming substitution with the bindings
for its two endpoints before translating the body.

The expression translation $\USTransExpr{\E}$ in \textsc{Tr-Exp} takes
only the expression $\E$ and the substitution $\Sub$.  It replaces free
variables according to $\Sub$ and preserves constants and expression
constructors, including application directions and type annotations.
Under an expression binder the substitution is lifted to map the bound
variable to itself, avoiding capture: once for abstraction, recursion, and
each case branch, and twice for a pair-elimination body.  In particular,
$\CSSplit$ translates directly to the unary target constant
$\USConst{rsplit}$, also in first-class uses.

The initial flag lists follow source separator order, whereas remote split
allocates each new cell at the front.  Synchronization depends on the
pre- and post-references in the handles, so physical storage order can vary
while preserving the same dependencies.  In the positional representation
used by Agda, front insertion shifts every existing reference on that
endpoint by one throughout the expression multiset, including inside
closures and evaluation contexts.  Acquire removes its referenced cell and
renumbers the surviving references.  With the named references used in
\cref{fig:unr-process-reduction}, both updates preserve the names of surviving
cells.  The image relation in \cref{sec:simulation} accounts for these
storage choices by consistent flag permutations.
